Notes

Why use a transcript instead of a recording bot

A bot joining a call changes the meeting: someone has to approve the recording, participants get a notice, and in Germany or under a strict client contract that alone can end the conversation. Every major platform already writes a transcript for free. Using it is the low-friction route — and the only one that works for a call that has already happened.

What the notes contain

Four buckets and nothing else: decided (with the condition attached), action (person, verb, date), open (the question nobody answered), context (one line of why). If a sentence was a musing rather than a decision, it belongs in open — treating “we should probably…” as agreement is how teams circulate commitments nobody made.

FAQ

Is there a free tool that turns a Zoom transcript into meeting notes?

Yes — this one. Download the .vtt from Zoom, drop it above, and you get decisions, owners and open items without an account or a bot in the call.

Can I paste a .vtt or .srt file directly?

Yes. The timestamps and cue numbers are stripped in your browser, and only the clean text is sent off to be summarised.

Does a bot have to join my meeting?

No. This is text-only — you bring the transcript the platform already made, so nobody sees a recorder notice.

Will speaker names survive?

If the transcript labels speakers, yes. “Speaker 2” stays “Speaker 2”, so relabel before you send the notes out.
