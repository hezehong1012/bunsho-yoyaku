What to do with the draft in the next 20 minutes

Search the target query and read the top three results' opening 100 words. Whatever they all say, say it faster. Whatever none of them say — a real number, a screenshot, a failure mode — add it. That gap is the reason a page ranks.

Then cut the intro to two sentences, because the draft will have written three paragraphs of throat-clearing. Keep the FAQ: it is the cheapest way to pick up long-tail queries.

Honest limits

The draft has no data of its own, so anything that sounds like a statistic is a placeholder. Titles that promise a number (“7 ways…”) invite readers to check whether the seven are real. Publish only what you can stand behind.

FAQ

Is this publish-ready?

No. It gives structure, a first answer and an FAQ shell. Add your own numbers, examples and screenshots before publishing.

Will it rank on Google?

Structure helps; ranking needs specifics this tool cannot invent. Read the top three results, then answer better.

What belongs in the brief?

The search question, who is asking, and anything specific you already know. More specifics, less generic draft.
