Summary

Why copy-paste instead of upload

The three-step version takes twenty seconds and avoids the two things that make PDF tools fail: scanned pages with no text layer, and two-column layouts where the reading order gets scrambled. When you copy the text yourself you see what the model sees, so a strange summary is explained by a strange paste rather than by a mystery.

Most PDF readers give you the whole document in one selection. If the text comes out interleaved from two columns, select column by column, or use the “copy as text” option some readers hide under Export.

What it is good at

RFP responses where you need the requirements and the caveats. Analyst notes where the numbers matter more than the narrative. Contracts and policy documents where you want the obligations in plain English before you read the whole thing.

FAQ

Can I upload the PDF file directly?

Not yet. Extracting and OCR-ing a PDF is a different engineering job, and it is what paid PDF tools charge for. Here you paste the text; the summary stays free.

What about scanned PDFs?

If you can select the text in your viewer, it can be summarized. If the page is a photo of paper, copying gives you nothing — run OCR first.

A 40-page report will not fit, right?

The limit is 15,000 characters, about 20 dense pages. Summarize the sections you actually need, or feed the executive summary and the conclusion together.
