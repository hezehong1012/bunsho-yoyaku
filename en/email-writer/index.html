The three-line brief that works best

Who it is to, what happened, and the one action you want. Add a deadline if there is one, and a reason if the reader will wonder why. That is enough context for a draft that does not read like a template.

If you already have a subject line you like, put it in the brief — the draft will keep it. If the tone matters more than the content (a complaint, a rejection), say so explicitly; “firm but not rude” is a usable instruction.

What it will not do

It does not invent facts, discounts, or commitments. If the brief is vague, the email will be vague, because guessing at your commercial terms is exactly how these tools get people into trouble. Add the numbers yourself if they matter.

FAQ

Will it sound like a bot?

It is prompted against the usual tells — no “hope this finds you well”, no stacked buzzwords. Add one concrete detail of your own and it reads human.

Can it write a hard email?

Yes. Name the situation and the outcome you want. Read it twice before sending — tone is the one thing you cannot undo.

Does it send the email?

No. It only drafts text you copy. No mailbox, no contacts, no sending.
