Summarizing research papers and PDFs without uploading them

Two things stop people from using AI on papers: the file upload, and the feeling that a summary of a method section is worthless. Both are solvable.

The privacy part

If the paper is under embargo, licensed, or contains participant data, uploading it to an unknown service is a compliance decision, not a convenience one. Copying the text out and pasting it into a tool that stores nothing is a smaller exposure — and the one to argue for if you have to justify it.

Summarize in three parts, not one

A whole paper summarized at once gives you the abstract back. Instead, paste the abstract and conclusion together first — that tells you whether to continue. Then paste the method section alone and ask what was actually measured and what was assumed. Then the results, and ask for the numbers with their units.

The question that saves the most time

“What would have to be true for this result to be wrong?” Ask that of the discussion section. Papers are usually honest about their limits; the limits are what determine whether the paper is relevant to your situation, and they are what a generic summary drops first.

Reading order for a literature sweep

Title and abstract, then the conclusion, then the limitations paragraph, then the figures. Only if all four survive do you read the method. With summaries of the first three, that triage takes two minutes per paper instead of twenty.

What not to do

Do not cite a paper you have only had summarized. Do not copy a sentence from a summary into your own draft — you will have paraphrased the summarizer, not the authors.
