Turning a meeting transcript into decisions and owners

A transcript is a record of everything said. Notes are a record of what will happen. Between them sits the work almost nobody enjoys: deciding which sentence was a decision, which was a musing, and which was a task waiting for a name.

Four buckets, nothing else

Decided. Something is different from last week because of this meeting. Include the condition attached to it.

Action. A person, a verb, a date. If any of the three is missing, it is not an action yet — it goes in open items.

Open. The question someone asked that nobody answered. These are the most valuable lines in the document and the first ones a summary tool flattens.

Context. One or two lines of why, for anyone who missed it.

The signal words to watch for

“So we're going with…” is a decision. “Can you get me that by Thursday?” is an action. “We should probably…” is not a decision, however confidently it was said. If your notes treat that third pattern as agreement, you will circulate a commitment nobody made — the single most common failure of AI-generated minutes.

Getting a usable transcript in the first place

Zoom writes an audio transcript you can download; Teams keeps one in the meeting chat; Meet drops it in the host's Drive. Named speakers matter more than perfect punctuation — with “Speaker 2” in the transcript you get “Speaker 2” in the notes.
